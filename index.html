Templates 05–08
05 LinkedIn Connection Requests
Rules for connection requests
  • 300 character limit — be concise, warm, and specific
  • Never ask for a job in the connection request itself
  • Reference something specific about them — shows you did homework
  • After they accept, wait 24–48 hours then send your main message

To a Recruiter (Tech)

Connection Request
Hi [Name], I noticed you recruit for engineering roles at [Company]. I'm a MERN/Next.js developer actively exploring remote opportunities with international companies. Would love to be on your radar. Happy to connect!
Keep it simple. Don't mention salary or specific roles yet.

To an Engineering Manager

Connection Request
Hi [Name], I've been following [Company]'s engineering work — the way your team built [specific feature] is impressive. I'm a full-stack MERN dev exploring remote roles. Would love to connect and learn more about your work.
Mention something specific — a blog post, a product feature, a GitHub repo. Generic messages get ignored.

To a Developer at a Target Company

Connection Request
Hi [Name], saw your post about [topic]. Really resonated — I had a similar experience building [X]. Would love to connect with other devs working on interesting products. No agenda, just building my network!
Developers are your best internal referral sources. Be genuine, not salesy.

To a CTO / Founder

Connection Request
Hi [Name], I've been using [Product] for [X months] — really love how you've handled [specific feature]. I'm a MERN/Next.js developer who's built similar products and would love to connect with founders building in this space.
Being a real user of their product is the strongest possible opening. Mention it if true.

12 Salary Negotiation Scripts

Script 1 — When asked your expectations (early in process)

Negotiation · Early stage
Interviewer: "What are your salary expectations?" You: "Based on my research into market rates for senior MERN/Next.js developers in remote roles with [US / UK] companies, and given my experience with [TypeScript, real-time systems, SaaS architecture], I'm targeting $[X]–$[Y]K per year. That said, I'm genuinely more interested in the right role and team than hitting a specific number. I'm flexible depending on the full package — equity, learning opportunities, and growth path all matter to me. What's the budget range you're working with for this position?" 💡 Tip: Always ask what their range is. Never be the only one who shows their cards.
Give a range where your minimum is 10% above your actual minimum. Let them anchor high.

Script 2 — Counter-offer (offer came in low)

Negotiation · Counter-offer
Them: "We'd like to offer you $[X]K" You: "Thank you so much — I'm genuinely excited about this role and the team. I've really enjoyed the interview process and I'm very motivated to join. I did want to have an honest conversation about the compensation. Based on my research and what I bring specifically — particularly [TypeScript migration experience / real-time systems / SaaS architecture] — I was expecting something closer to $[X+15]K. Is there any flexibility on the base, or alternatively, could we add [equity / signing bonus / extra PTO / 3-month salary review] to bridge the gap?" 💡 Tip: Always counter at least once. 80% of employers expect it and have room to go higher.
Be warm but firm. Never accept the first offer on the call — say you need to review and respond in 24–48 hours.

Script 3 — Using competing offer as leverage

Negotiation · Competing offer
You: "I want to be fully transparent with you — I do have another offer on the table for $[Y]K from [Company type, e.g. 'a US SaaS startup']. I want to be honest because [Company] is genuinely my first choice — the product, team, and mission align much more closely with what I'm looking for. Is there any way you could match or come close to $[Y]K? That would make this an easy decision for me." 💡 Tip: Only use this if the competing offer is real. Don't bluff — it can backfire badly.
Apply to multiple companies simultaneously so you genuinely have competing offers to leverage.
